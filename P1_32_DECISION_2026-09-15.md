# P1.32 Gate Decision — Funding Cross-Section Panel (perpetual-only)

Status: **not justified under the frozen promotion gates**
Date: 2026-09-15
Scope: Binance USD-M USDT perpetuals, weekly decisions at the Sunday close, long the
lowest-funding quintile and short the highest-funding quintile of the P1.27 universe, ranked
on trailing one- or four-week funding, held one or four weeks, no spot leg

## Decision

Family `funding_xs_panel_v1` is rejected. None of the four pre-registered members is eligible
for further review. The decision report records `no_eligible_member` and the final holdout
remains locked. No live, paper, or shadow authority follows from this.

The family was declared on 2026-09-15 in
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-15-funding-xs-family-design.md` before any fold ran, and
evaluated through the P1.27 pipeline with one declared change: the adverse funding rule is
P1.28's (receipts ×0.75, payments ×2) instead of P1.27's (receipts ×0). The controls confirm
the shared path: `random_ranks` and `passive_long_ew` reproduce P1.27's and P1.31's base
totals to the last digit (−0.50973 and −0.35137), and their adverse totals differ from those
records by exactly the declared funding rule.

The three dollar-neutral members earn their funding — 21 to 29 bps per week received net of
payments, against 5 to 9 bps of trading cost — and are base-positive at 11 to 22 bps per
week. None of them can be told from zero (raw one-sided p 0.13 to 0.25, bootstrap lower
bounds below zero, Benjamini-Hochberg q 0.25 across four), each carries 54 to 63% of its
pooled profit in one fold, and all three are adverse-negative. The fourth member, with the
sign-flip exit rule, is the first candidate in this repository that is positive under both
cost scenarios and passes the fold, concentration and dominance gates; it fails the two
statistical gates, and its price return is a net-short market position, not the quintile
spread the family hypothesised (deviation 4).

- Signal: `F_L(c, t)` = sum of contract *c*'s funding settlements in `(t − L weeks, t]`

  score. Every eligible contract was rankable in every test week of every fold (each leg
  held one fifth of the 100-contract universe, 40 held of 100 eligible).
- Book: P1.27's quintile construction with the ranking reversed — long the lowest-funding
  fifth, short the highest-funding fifth, equal weight within each leg, each leg one half of
  gross, minimum quintile size 8. Four-week members hold four overlapping weekly cohorts of
  one quarter of capital (P1.31's cohort book, three-Sunday warm-up).
- Members: `fx_q5_l4w_h1w` (lookback 4, hold 1), `fx_q5_l4w_h4w` (4, 4), `fx_q5_l1w_h4w`
  (1, 4), `fx_q5_l4w_h4w_exit` (4, 4, exit rule: at every decision a held long whose trailing
  one-week funding is ≥ 0, or a held short whose trailing one-week funding is ≤ 0, is removed
  from every cohort that holds it; its share stays undeployed until the cohort ages out).
- Costs: fee 5 bps per side, slippage 5/10 bps by tier (doubled under adverse), funding from
  actual settlements — base receipts ×1 / payments ×1, adverse receipts ×0.75 / payments ×2
  — forced closes ×1 / ×2. Bootstrap 2,000 × block 4 × seed 17, BH q ≤ 0.10 over four members.

Three observations.

1. **The funding is real and the price spread is not.** The neutral books collect 21 to 29
   bps per week of funding net of payments, three to five times their trading cost. Their
   price return — the quintile spread the literature reports as favourable — is +1.4 to −4.9
   bps per week: at a weekly cadence over 2021–2025, rich-funding names did not underperform
   cheap-funding names by anything the book could keep.
2. **The adverse rule bites through the long leg.** Solving base and adverse funding for
   gross receipts and payments: the four-week book receives about 33 bps per week and pays
   about 11; the lowest-funding quintile still has positive funding most weeks (mean four-week
   score +9 bps), so the long leg is a payer. Receipts ×0.75 and payments ×2 turn +21.7 into
   +2.4 bps, which the doubled slippage then consumes. Doubled slippage alone costs the
   four-week book 3.5 bps per week; the funding rule costs it 19. Measured execution costs
   are therefore not the lever for this family.
3. **The exit member is a market-timing position.** Its rule removes a long when that name's
   week of funding is non-negative and a short when it is non-positive. Because funding is
   positive far more often than negative, longs are removed about twice as often as shorts
   (2,467 removals over 225 weeks, about 11 per week), leaving a mean long of 0.21 against a
   mean short of 0.45 and a mean net exposure of −0.24 on a gross of 0.66. The member's price
   return of +16.5 bps per week is fully accounted for by that net exposure times the
   equal-weight universe's return in the same weeks (+21.6 bps per week): the book is net
   short precisely after weeks of broadly positive funding, and those weeks were followed by
   declines. Its payments fall to about 4 bps per week because the paying longs are exactly
   the ones removed, which is why it survives the adverse rule. That is a contrarian
   funding-sentiment timing bet with the funding receipt on top — not the dollar-neutral
   quintile spread declared in the hypothesis, and not distinguishable from zero on its own
   terms (p 0.112, bootstrap lower −0.0025, fold 5 −0.216 under base).

## What this answers

A perpetual-only funding cross-section on this universe pays its own turnover and keeps a
funding premium under base costs, but the premium is not statistically distinguishable from
zero on 225 weeks, is concentrated in 2021 (folds 0–1) and early 2025 (fold 7), and does not
survive the declared adverse funding rule because the long leg pays. This joins P1.28 and
P1.29 as the third family in which funding is a real gross source and the declared adverse
treatment is what defeats it. The difference from those two is instructive: the spot-hedged
carry lost to the hedge leg's execution cost, which the Binance cost journal (P1.30) can
measure; the perpetual-only cross-section loses to the funding asymmetry itself, which no
cost measurement changes.

The exit member says what a viable funding book would have to look like: a leg is worth
holding only while it is paid. But the way this member gets there — dropping the paying
longs and leaving the capital undeployed — converts the book into a timed net-short
position whose profit came from the market, not from the spread. Any declaration that builds
on it must be dollar-neutral by construction (equal leg sizes after the filter, or an explicit
beta hedge), report net exposure as a gate-relevant extra, and be evaluated on the price and
funding components separately.

## Declared deviations and disclosures

1. **Design informed by earlier records** (spec section 2): the family recombines what
   P1.28, P1.29 and P1.31 showed on the same nine test windows — a real funding premium,
   the cost of the spot leg, and the cross-sectional construction's price effect. It is not
   tuned to any fold; every rule was fixed in the spec before a fold ran. The holdout was
   never read.
2. **Warm-up.** Each fold forms weight vectors on the three Sundays before its first test
   decision (no episode, nothing carried); reason code
   `FOLD_OPENING_BOOK_WARMED_FROM_PRIOR_WEEKS`, `warm_up_weeks: 3` in every fold report. The
   pooled decision artifact carries no fold reason codes; this record does.
3. **Post-run precision fix (commit 6d358df).** The first chain, at the merge `aa3a7c7`,
   produced fold 0 and stopped at fold 1: the per-episode net-return summation in
   `panel_accounting` is done under an exact context that traps any rounding, and the
   four-week cohort book leaves residual weights of about 1e-28 (a long in an older cohort
   nearly cancelling a short in a newer one) whose contributions extend the exact sum to 55
   significant digits in fold 1 (`fx_q5_l1w_h4w`, base, week of 2021-11-07; smallest term
   1.8e-31), beyond the 50-digit headroom. The headroom was raised to 120 digits, matching
   the pooling context raised for P1.31. An exact sum is the same at any sufficient
   precision: before the chain was re-run, P1.31's trend fold 0 was re-derived at the new
   headroom to values identical to its bound report in every field except `code_hash` and
   `report_hash` (kept at `artifacts/verify-prec120/`), and the pinned trend fold-1 fixture
   test still holds. All nine P1.32 folds and the decision were then produced at `6d358df`;
   the fold 0 from `aa3a7c7` agrees with the new fold 0 in every field except those two
   hashes and is kept at `artifacts/fxs/stale-prec50/`. No rule, threshold or number moved.
4. **The exit member is not dollar-neutral.** The spec's hypothesis (section 4.1) describes
   a dollar-neutral book; its exit rule (section 3) as written removes legs one at a time and
   leaves the capital undeployed, so the member's mean net exposure is −0.24 (long 0.21,
   short 0.45). The rule was applied exactly as declared. Had this member passed, it would
   have been eligible only with this caveat attached; see "Where the money goes".
5. **Adverse funding rule** differs from P1.27's by declaration (spec section 4): receipts
   ×0.75 and payments ×2 instead of receipts ×0. The controls' base totals equal P1.27's and
   P1.31's to the digit; their adverse totals differ by exactly this rule.
6. **Union removal across cohorts.** A contract removed by the exit rule is removed from
   every retained cohort that holds it at that decision, and each cohort's formed size is
   unchanged, so the freed share stays uninvested until the cohort ages out (mean gross
   exposure 0.66). The count `exit_rule_removals` is per episode across all cohorts.
7. **Out-of-universe cohort holdings** of the four-week members exit at tier-two slippage
   (`panel_accounting`'s default), as in P1.31.
8. **Funding-dump gap after 2025-06.** Binance's monthly funding-rate dumps stop after
   2025-06 for about 126 of the roughly 480 live perpetuals (confirmed at the bucket on
   2026-09-15). It did not reach this family's test windows: every eligible contract was
   rankable in every week of fold 8 (both quintiles held 20 of 100). It does reach the locked
   holdout for any affected name that is in the liquid universe there, so a holdout read of
   any funding-based family (P1.28, P1.29, P1.30, this one) needs a fresh capture first.
9. The decision JSON's `declared_deviations` block carries P1.27's texts (rankable subset,
   held-nothing rolling), which apply here unchanged; the deviations specific to this family
   are recorded only in this document, which is authoritative.
10. Re-running any P1.27, P1.28, P1.29 or P1.31 fold now yields a different `code_hash`
    (`panel_accounting.py` changed); their bound artifacts remain the only poolable sets for
    those families.

## Revisit condition

No revisit of this family. A funding book on this panel is next declarable only as a new
family that is dollar-neutral by construction after any funding filter, reports net exposure
per episode, and separates price from funding in its primary read; measured perpetual tiers
from the Binance journal may replace the assumed slippage but do not address the failure
recorded here. P1.30 (spot-hedged carry on measured costs) proceeds as planned once the
journal completes. The CTM mixture-of-experts line stays `research_only`: there is still no
base with an edge under the declared adverse costs.
