# P1.30 Gate Decision — Funding Carry v3 (v2's book on measured execution costs)

Status: **not justified under the frozen promotion gates**
Date: 2026-09-22
Scope: long Binance spot / short USD-M USDT perpetual pairs, weekly decisions, overlapping
cohorts held 13 or 26 weeks, exit rule and cost hurdle as declared for P1.29; slippage tiers
read from the Binance cost journal's finalisation receipt under the rule fixed in the

## Decision

Family `funding_carry_panel_v3` is rejected. None of the four members is eligible for
further review. The decision report records `no_eligible_member` and the final holdout
remains locked. No live, paper, or shadow authority follows from this.

The family is P1.29's declaration with exactly one change: the four slippage values are
measured, not assumed. Base tiers fell from 5/10 to 1/4 bps per side and adverse tiers from
10/20 to 3/16. That closed most of P1.29's shortfall — the strongest member,
`carry_l4w_h26w_exit`, went from −0.7 bps per week under adverse costs to −0.03 — and it
was not enough. Under the adverse funding rule (receipts ×0.75, payments ×2) the book's
funding income and its doubled slippage cancel to the basis point. The spot-hedged carry at
5,000 / 50,000 USDT order sizes is closed at this venue.

## What this answers

P1.28 asked whether the spot-hedged funding carry is real; it is (statistically, in every
declaration since). P1.29 asked whether forecast-free rules can carry it over the adverse
floor; they could not, and the record deferred to measurement. P1.30 is that measurement:
with the actual Binance book at 5,000 and 50,000 USDT per order, the v2 book is a coin
flip under adverse costs and the coin landed on −0.03 bps per week. The gap is not
execution at this size — it is that the exit-rule cohort book leaves 42% of capital
undeployed (mean gross 0.58) while the doubled slippage is charged on every re-entry.

The question this closes is "does the large-book carry pay its own execution at standard
taker fees"; the answer at this venue and scale is no. Whether the *small* book does, at the
order sizes the user would actually place, is P1.33 (`P1_33_DECISION_2026-09-22.md`), run
the same day on the same receipt.

## Declared deviations and disclosures

1. **Receipt taken at the eligibility floor, not the declared target.** The journal spec
   declares 11,000 rounds; its eligibility floors are 10,000 observations at 5,000 USDT
   over at least 7 days with at least four contributors per tier and market. On 2026-09-17,
   before the receipt existed, the chain trigger was lowered from the 11,000-round target to
   the 10,000-segment floor (the extra 1,000 were a null-rate buffer and the observed null
   rate was zero). The receipt was finalised at 10,003 segments, every instrument eligible,
   every tier median with 7 or 8 contributors. The journal kept running afterwards toward
   its target; the receipt is immutable and the declarations cite it by hash.
2. **The journal's sample is not uniform in time.** It ran from 2026-09-12 11:53 UTC to
   the receipt's last segment at 2026-09-22 14:54 UTC, 10.13 days, in which 14,342 rounds
   were possible at 61 seconds and 10,003 were taken: about 30% of the wall clock was lost
   to the machine sleeping on idle (disabled 2026-09-17), an aborted shutdown that killed
   the supervisor from 2026-09-17 02:37 to 13:04, and further restarts on 09-18, 09-20
   (twice) and 09-21. Gaps skip rounds; they do not alter any observation. The tier medians
   are therefore medians over a sample that under-represents some hours of some days.
3. **First finalisation refused; the receipt came from the second, 59 seconds later.** The
   P1.30 chain's `finalize` at 16:54:19 local was refused with
   `CHAIN_HEAD_COUNT_MISMATCH, CHAIN_HEAD_SEQUENCE_MISMATCH, CHAIN_HEAD_LINK_MISMATCH`: the
   journal was appending a segment while the verifier read the chain head. The P1.33 chain,
   started by the watcher on that exit, finalised at 16:55:36 without complaint (10,003
   segments); the P1.30 chain was re-run at 17:49:49 against that receipt and skipped the
   finalisation step. `binance-cost-journal-status` reports `verify: ok` at 10,196 segments.
   Nothing about the journal, the rule or the receipt was changed.
4. **The result was previewed before the receipt existed.** On 2026-09-15 a read-only
   preview of the tier medians at 3,580 rounds was taken (never published), and on
   2026-09-17 a provisional chain was run on an assumed-cost declaration carrying the
   preview's declared-table equivalents (base 1/4, adverse 3/17 bps): it produced
   `no_eligible_member`, best adverse −0.00384. The receipt moved one number: the tier-2
   adverse value fell from 16.11 to 15.56 bps, so the declared adverse tier is 16, not 17,
   and the best member's adverse total moved from −0.00384 to −0.00074. The base tables were
   identical, and every base-scenario number here equals the provisional run's to the
   digit. The preview and the provisional run are cost data seen in advance; no rule,
   threshold or reading was chosen from them (the section-5 reading predates the journal).
5. **Code hash.** The fold reports' `code_hash` (`89b2fb19…`) differs from P1.29's
   (`16c64f3f…`): `panel_accounting`'s exact-sum headroom was raised from 50 to 120 digits
   (commit `6d358df`, disclosed in P1.32), the carry runner gained the slot-book mode
   (P1.33) and its decision loop was extracted for the holdout read (P1.34). The cohort
   path's output is pinned byte-for-byte by `tests/fixtures/carry_v1_fold0_expected.json`
   and the P1.31 re-derivation; the controls reproduce the provisional run exactly. P1.29's
   bound artifacts remain the only poolable set for that family.
6. The decision JSON's `declared_deviations` block still carries P1.27's texts (known label
   defect, unchanged since P1.28); this record is authoritative.
7. P1.29's disclosures 2 (exit rule strips the just-formed cohort), 3 (extras means), 5
   (P1.28 reproducibility) and 7 (2026 spot liquidity below the 40-pair floor) apply
   unchanged.
